# Learning design and outcomes

## Series outcomes

By the end of the Pine Ridge Hydrology series, learners should be able to:

1. Use a shared Python environment and an ordered notebook workflow.
2. Trace a figure or table from public source data through configuration and code.
3. Inspect spatial coverage, units, missingness, record length, and transformations.
4. Apply reproducible hydrologic methods without treating screening values as approved thresholds.
5. Separate a computed observation from interpretation, external evidence, and an authorized decision.
6. Explain why public discoverability does not remove Indigenous data-governance responsibilities.
7. Document limitations and contribute a small change through peer review.

## Instructional contract

The notebooks use public environmental data for education and screening. They do not contain OST operational data, establish OST or OLC conclusions, or replace local knowledge and decision authority. Statements about causes, infrastructure equity, health, regulation, or management require evidence beyond a computed indicator and the applicable review.

## Notebook design requirements

Every notebook includes:

- purpose and explicit learning objectives;
- prerequisites and an estimated duration;
- a governance checkpoint;
- prediction or framing questions;
- at least one learner checkpoint;
- an interpretation protocol;
- a small contribution activity;
- limitations and a connection to the next notebook.

Automated tests protect the presence of this structure. They do not assess instructional quality; facilitators and OLC/OST reviewers remain responsible for that judgment.

## Assessment approach

Assessment is artifact-based rather than syntax-based. Learners produce a short evidence record containing:

- one result they regenerated;
- the relevant source, unit, period, and spatial scope;
- one transformation they can explain;
- one limitation or alternative explanation;
- one question requiring local knowledge or additional evidence; and
- one documentation or code-review contribution.

## Module outcomes

| Notebook | Learners will be able to… |
|---|---|
| 01 | distinguish territorial, statistical, and watershed boundaries; inspect spatial overlap and monitoring context |
| 02 | assess groundwater record coverage and interpret depth-to-water trends cautiously |
| 03 | summarize seasonal streamflow and explain the status of screening thresholds |
| 04 | check units, benchmarks, and sampling coverage before interpreting water-quality flags |
| 05 | use a regional drought proxy and distinguish association from prediction or causation |
| 06 | explain normalization, missing components, weights, and sensitivity in a composite index |
| 07 | distinguish scenarios, projections, models, and forecasts and communicate uncertainty |

## Review and revision

The learning design should be reviewed after each delivery. Record where learners stopped, which terms required explanation, which cells failed, and which interpretations were routinely overstated. Revise the notebooks and facilitator guide rather than treating those difficulties as learner deficits.
